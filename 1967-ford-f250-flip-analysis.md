**The biggest red flag:** a 1967 F-250 could not have left the factory with a 351. The only 1967 V8 was the **352 FE** (VIN engine code **Y**); the other options were the 240 and 300 inline sixes (codes **A** and **B**). The 351 Windsor did not appear in F-series trucks until later model years. So either the seller is confusing 352 with 351 (very common, since the two share 4.00" × 3.50" bore and stroke), or the engine was swapped (351W, 351M or 400). Check the 5th character of the VIN and the engine itself before negotiating. See [Ford Authority](https://fordauthority.com/2020/12/this-1967-ford-f-250-features-light-modifications-and-a-healthy-patina/) and [Ford-Trucks forum: 1967 engine options](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/865505-67-engine-options.html).

**Do Boise buyers pay more or less than national buyers?**

* **For clean, rust-free, presentable trucks:** Boise is roughly at national private-party levels. Dry inland-Northwest climates produce a lot of solid trucks, so supply is decent.
* **For a rusty driver like this one:** **local buyers pay less than BaT-level prices.** (1) The dry climate means local buyers can find rust-free trucks, so rust gets penalized harder than in the Midwest. (2) National auction buyers pay for presentation and reach, but that premium mostly goes away below about $8k once you add the 5% buyer fee and $1,200–$2,000 in cross-country shipping. (3) The local buyer for a $4–5k F-250 is a practical buyer (farm, hauling, first project) who negotiates hard.
* **Estimated Treasure Valley private-party value:** **$3,500–$4,000 as-is**, **$4,500–$5,000 sorted**. *These are estimates built from the comps above. They are not verified local sale prices.*

**Should you sell it on BaT instead?** Only if it inspects well. A1 shows BaT will take a $6k-class project, but listing photography, the 5% buyer fee (paid by the buyer, which lowers bids), and a real chance of a no-sale like A5 make it a gamble. Local private sale is the conservative channel.

**Things that move the price:**
* **Engine.** An original, numbers-plausible 352 adds about $300–$700 of buyer confidence. A 351W or 351M swap is neutral to slightly negative for a driver buyer and negative for a purist. A mystery engine with leaks is −$500.
* **Trim.** Custom Cab, Ranger or Camper Special (dual tanks, heavier cooling, badges) adds $300–$1,000. Base trim adds nothing.
* **Floors and cab mounts.** If the cab-corner rust has spread into the floor pans and cab mounts (common, see risks), subtract $1,000–$2,000, and the truck becomes a project, not a driver.

## 5. Demand and selling time

* **How fast they sell:** a sorted, honestly described 1967–72 Ford truck priced around **$4,500–$5,000** in Boise typically draws strong interest on Facebook Marketplace within days. Deals usually close in **1–4 weeks in spring and summer**. Listings priced at "collector" levels ($9,500–$23,000) sit for months or years, as the Boise dealer and OldCarOnline listings above show. **Seasonality matters:** you would buy in November, and December–February are the slowest months for project and old trucks in Idaho (snow, holiday budgets). Budget for a **45–90 day** sale, or sell at the low end to hit 30–60 days. Listing in March–May would likely add $250–$500.
* **Likely buyers:** (1) a local hobbyist or first-time classic owner who wants a patina 1960s Ford they can drive now, (2) a farm or ranch buyer who wants a cheap 3/4-ton hauler, (3) a flipper or project builder who will lowball, and (4) a parts or donor buyer (grilles, the 4-speed, the 352 if original), who sets the floor around $2,000–$2,500.
* **Is the configuration desirable?** It's middling.
  * Plus: V8 with 4-speed manual (a "real truck" spec that enthusiasts like), first-year 1967 "bumpside" styling, clean title, runs and drives, dry-climate region.
  * Neutral: long bed. **Every 1967–72 F-250 has the 8-ft bed**, so that isn't a flaw against other F-250s, but short-bed F-100s are what most hobby buyers really want.
  * Minus: 2WD (the money in this generation is in 4x4 Highboys), probably base trim, visible rust, a likely non-original or mislabeled engine, a heavy 3/4-ton ride, probably no power steering, and drum brakes.

## 6. Risks (with typical costs)

| Area | What goes wrong | Inspection check | Cost if bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engine ID ("351")** | 1967 V8s were 352 FE only. A "351" is either a mislabeled 352 or a swap. | VIN 5th character (Y = 352, A/B = six). FE engines have the distributor at the front of the intake and wide, pent-roof valve covers. A Windsor has narrow covers and a front distributor. A 351M/400 is a wide-deck engine with the distributor at the front. | Collector value −$300 to −$700 if swapped. A swap done badly (wiring, mounts, cooling) costs $300–$1,000 to fix. |
| **352 FE health** | Rope-type rear main seal leaks (a proper fix needs the engine out), leaks from the intake and valve covers that look like a rear-main leak, worn guides and rings (smoke) ([Ford-Trucks forum](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/45625-352-oil-leak.html); [FE oil leak](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/1620902-fe-oil-leak-question.html)) | Cold start; look for blue smoke at startup and on deceleration; compression test if allowed; oil on the bellhousing | Reseal $300–800; rebuild $3,500–6,000 (kills the flip) |
| **4-speed (likely Warner T-18 or a New Process 435)** | **1st gear is unsynchronized** (6.32:1 "granny"), so grinding into 1st is normal. Worn 2–3–4 synchros, popping out of gear, bearing whine ([Bullnose Garage T-18 guide](https://bullnosegarage.com/borg-warner-t18-transmission-for-ford-trucks-1980-1986/)) | Shift through every gear on a test drive, under load and coasting. Check for clutch slip in 4th. | Clutch $600–1,200 (shop); trans rebuild $800–1,500 |
| **Cab corners → floor pans → cab mounts** | Cab corners, floors, rockers and cab mounts rust together. Mounts trap moisture and rot the floor ([Ford-Trucks forum](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/419678-floor-pans-and-front-cab-mount-replacement.html)). | Pull the floor mats; probe the floors and the area under the seat; look at the cab mounts from underneath; check the rockers and the door-bottom drain holes. | Parts: cab corners $30–80/side, floor pans $150–400, complete floor assembly with mounts about $1,350 ([CJ Pony Parts](https://www.cjponyparts.com/complete-cab-floor-assembly-low-hump-with-mount-rockers-crossmember-f-100-1967-972/p/FLP42/)). Shop labor makes it $800–$6,000. **Floors or mounts rotted = walk away at $2,600.** |
| **Frame** | Usually robust, but check the rear spring hangers, the area under the battery tray, and bed mounts. Make sure the VIN stamped on the frame matches the title. | Hammer tap and visual check | Frame rot = walk away |
| **Brakes** | All drums on a 3/4-ton. Leaking wheel cylinders, cracked rubber hoses, an old master cylinder, seized adjusters | Pedal feel, pull under braking, wet backing plates | $300–800 for a full refresh |
| **Steering and front end** | Twin I-Beam pivot and radius-arm bushings wear together; kingpins are hard to replace; play in the steering box; wander; cupped tires ([Ford-Trucks forum](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/1342961-having-trouble-finding-radius-arm-bushing-install-tips.html), [Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/1842219/ford-twin-i-beam-front-suspension-design/)) | Jack up the front and rock the wheel at 12 and 6 o'clock (kingpins) and 3 and 9 (tie rods); watch the bushings | Bushings plus kingpins $400–1,000 at a shop; alignment $100–150 |
| **Parts availability** | **Excellent.** LMC Truck, Dennis Carpenter, National Parts Depot, CJ Pony Parts and Eckler's all stock 1967–72 parts. Mechanical parts are cheap; the expense is body and paint labor. | — | — |

## 7. Negotiation and inspection checklist

1. **Before you go:** ask the seller for the VIN. Characters 1–3 should be **F25** (F-250 2WD). Character 4 is the plant. Character 5 is the engine (**Y = 352 V8**). Ask why they say "351".
2. **On site:** cold start, test-drive through all four gears, inspect the floors and cab mounts from underneath, check the frame VIN against the title, look for leaks, test the brakes.
3. **Offer:** **open at $1,800**, using the carb rebuild, ignition switch, cab-corner rust, engine ID question and winter timing as leverage. Target **$2,000**, ceiling **$2,200**. Above **$2,400**, walk away unless the floors and mounts are solid *and* the engine is an original 352 or the trim is Custom Cab or Ranger.
4. **After purchase:** title it in your name. Idaho requires transfer within 30 days or a $20 penalty applies, and if you resell vehicles often, check Idaho's dealer-licensing rules. Fix the carb and ignition, detail it, photograph it well, and list it at **$5,500 firm-ish, expecting $4,750**.
